TAKTO ONE companion app: UI design exploration, designs 31 to 50
================================================================

This file is append-only. Once a design is committed its entry is frozen.
Mistakes are corrected in the next design, never covered up in a previous one.

LESSONS FROM 1-30
-----------------
What worked. One stage, one subject: the hand alone, nothing drawn on top of
it, and the detail hidden until asked for. The honest feed badge on every
screen. Light tabular numerals for the summary. The decision that the accent
is spent on state, not on decoration. The capture harness: a pinned clock and
a URL that pins the screen, so every still is reproducible.

What did not. The twin was flat: roughness 0.92, no clearcoat, no specular,
no contact shadow, and the ACES transform on top of a matte tone turned every
colour into the same dusty toy plastic. Thirty looks were thirty colour swaps
of that one recipe, and none of them changed the light, the lens or the
stage. The hand was also framed too small and too high, floating in a black
field with nothing to relate to. The chrome was weak: glass at 7 percent
white over black is a grey smudge, the round icon buttons could be from any
app, the picker chips were generic Feather icons in pills, the nav a three
icon capsule. Nothing said TAKTO. Nothing asked to be touched.

What the twin needs to read as a precise instrument on a phone. A lens, not
a game camera: 24 to 32 degrees, close, the hand filling at least half the
short axis. Real specular behaviour: a clearcoat or a metal, a roughness in
the 0.3 to 0.55 band, an environment to reflect, and a rim light separating
the silhouette from the stage. Material contrast INSIDE the object: the
finger members in one material, the encoder boards and pins in a second, the
palm block in a third, so the mechanism reads as assembled parts rather than
as a single cast blob. A ground relationship: a contact shadow, a reflection,
a grid, a horizon, or a paper edge, so the hand has somewhere to be. Motion
that means something: the twin moves because the joints moved.

What I will do differently. Every design starts from the twin: material,
light, camera and stage are decided and looked at before any chrome is
drawn. Every design changes stage, type, colour, layout AND navigation, not
one of them. Controls are drawn as objects a hand recognises (a key, a
dial, a toggle, a rail, a tab of paper, a bezel) with real contrast and a
real pressed state, never a translucent pill with an icon in it. Fonts are
chosen per design and Inter is not used again. Every still is read at full
size before it is committed.

---- DESIGN 31: Rams panel ----
Thesis: A machine hand is an instrument, so show it the way Braun showed a radio: in a window cut into warm grey enamel, with keys you could find with your eyes closed.
Twin: Satin white members (roughness 0.34-0.40, clearcoat 0.5) with dark steel pins and graphite encoder boards, so the mechanism reads as assembled parts. A softbox environment high left, one hard warm key with a 2048 shadow map, a cool strip rim behind right, a floor bounce. The hand stands on a shadow-catching ground in a recessed window the colour of the panel, one step darker, with an inset shade along its top edge. A 27 degree lens at 1.9 units, elevation 30, and the new fitAspect rule so a short window moves the camera in rather than losing the hand. It reads as an instrument because it is photographed like a product: one key, one rim, one shadow, and its own contrasting parts.
UI: A panel below the window. A status strip with an amber LED and the words SYNTHETIC FEED on every screen. Keys with a face, a 2px side and a soft fall, that push down when pressed; the power key is charcoal with a green LED; three wide keys at the foot are the navigation. Mean flexion is a red needle on a printed scale beside a 34pt number; the four fingers and effort are segmented level meters with a red band at the top; the JOINTS key flips the panel into a 4x3 matrix of arc dials with needles that have mass (spring). Replay is a tape transport: a printed effort trace with a red playhead you drag, PAUSE as the dark key, EJECT, a ridged rotary with three detents for speed. Data prints the wire names on value tiles and the four rates as a table. Archivo throughout, small, tracked, upper case for silkscreen, tabular numerals.
Different from every previous design because: closest to the baseline in subject only. The stage is a light enamel panel, not black; nothing is translucent; the controls are physical keys, meters, needles and a rotary rather than glass pills; navigation is a key row; the type is Archivo not Inter; the twin is a white product shot with a real shadow, not a matte tone floating on black.
Why this is the best possible UI: every reading is an instrument you already know how to read (scale, meter, dial, tape), every control is a thing you already know how to press, and the hand is shown as what it is: a white precision product on a table. It ages well; nothing here depends on a trend.
What I would do better next time: The welcome leaves a band of empty panel under the keys. The dials could carry printed limits at the red band. Skip glyph keys need drawn triangles, not text characters.

---- DESIGN 32: Editorial plate ----
Thesis: The hand as a plate in a book: a printed page you read downward, a serif headline, tables with rules, and a numbered rail in the margin for navigation.
Twin: A lithograph, not a photograph. MeshToon shading with a three-step ramp (95, 175, 250) so every cylinder carries a flat highlight, a flat mid and a flat shade; a black outline drawn by an inverted hull pushed 0.36 percent of the model size along the vertex normals, added as a child of every rigged mesh so it follows the kinematics; members pale warm grey, the palm one step darker, the encoder boards near black, the pins pale. No environment, no tone mapping, no shadow: the page is the stage and the outline is what anchors the figure. A 30 degree lens at 1.6, elevation 22, six degrees of roll, and 60 percent of the feed's wrist roll performed by the whole figure so the plate breathes. It reads as a precise instrument because every edge is drawn, the way a patent drawing is.
UI: One long page with the plate pinned at the top (sticky header) and four sections under it: Cover, Live, Replay, Data. A rail down the left margin carries four numbered marks spread over the full height; the current one is inked and any one scrolls you there. Fraunces only: 44pt light headline with an italic turn, 72pt light tabular numeral for mean flexion, italic captions under every figure and table ("Fig. 1", "Table 1"), tracked capitals for labels. Controls are printed objects: black bars with a drawn arrow for the calls to action, words underlined in ink for choices, ink discs with drawn transport glyphs. Effort is an inked line; the joint table has a heavy rule and light rules; the take list is a contents page with numbers. Printer's red is spent on the playhead, a live dot and any value near its limit.
Different from every previous design because: 31 is a panel of physical keys on grey enamel with a white photographed hand; this is a paper page with a drawn hand, serif type, no keys, no window, and a scroll-and-rail navigation instead of a key row. The baseline is black glass; this has no glass and no black stage.
Why this is the best possible UI: reading numbers is reading, and a page is the most legible thing ever designed for it. The figure and its caption make the twin's provenance explicit on every screen. Nothing is chrome; everything is content.
What I would do better next time: The toon ramp could use a fourth step for the palm block. The cover headline wraps awkwardly at "hand,"; set it in two deliberate lines. Sections could carry a running page number at the foot.

---- DESIGN 33: Blueprint sheet ----
Thesis: The twin is a drawing set: a cyanotype sheet with a grid, zone marks, a title block, and index tabs on the bottom edge that are the navigation.
Twin: White line work on Prussian blue. The body wears the blue of the sheet one shade lighter (MeshStandard, roughness 0.9) so the volumes still turn; the silhouette is a white inverted hull at 0.28 percent of the model size; every crease of the CAD is drawn by EdgesGeometry at a 30 degree threshold, added as a child of each rigged mesh so the lines articulate with the joints. A 16 degree lens at 3.3 units keeps the projection nearly orthographic, the way a drawing is. No environment, no shadow. It reads as a precise instrument because it is presented as its own engineering drawing: every hole, boss and slide is a line.
UI: The screen is a sheet: an 8pt and 40pt grid, a one-pixel border, zone letters A-D across and 1-3 down the drawing, a VIEW note in the corner, a title block at the foot with TITLE, NOTE, SHEET n OF 4 and REV, and four index tabs hanging off the bottom edge that select the sheet. JetBrains Mono throughout, tracked capitals. Mean flexion is a dimension: end ticks, a line, the value in the middle. Each finger is a dimension bar with quarter ticks. Detail is TABLE 1, a ruled table; Data is TABLE 2 (channels, wire name against mechanical name) and TABLE 3 (rates). Replay's library is a revision table with LOAD callouts; the transport is a strip chart on the grid with a yellow playhead you drag. Controls are dashed callout boxes with drafting corners; the primary is filled white; pressing any of them turns it highlighter yellow.
Different from every previous design because: 31 is grey enamel with keys and a photographed white hand; 32 is a paper page with a toon drawing and a serif; this is a saturated blue field with white line work, a mono face, tables instead of lists, dimensions instead of numerals, and tabs on the sheet edge instead of a key row or a rail.
Why this is the best possible UI: the app is literally the documentation of a mechanism, and a drawing sheet is the form engineers already trust for exactly that. Everything on screen has a place a draughtsman would recognise, and the twin is finally shown with all of its detail visible instead of smoothed away by shading.
What I would do better next time: The cover's drawing could be larger. The MEASURES column wraps in Table 2; a narrower wire column would fix it. A dimension leader from the drawing to the live value would close the loop between figure and number.

---- DESIGN 34: HUD ----
Thesis: A head-up display: a hologram of the hand turning over a radar rose, readouts bracketed into the corners, and a radial dial at the foot as the navigation.
Twin: A fresnel hologram written as a ShaderMaterial: faces toward the viewer are nearly clear, faces turning away glow phosphor green, additive over black with depth writes off, so the hand is drawn by its own silhouettes and by every place one part passes in front of another. Members glow mint, pins white, the palm and encoder boards a deeper green at a lower base so the structure reads in layers. The opacity carries a slow beat and a faint fast flicker, and the whole hand turns on a slow turntable, because a projection rotates. 26 degree lens, elevation 24. It reads as a precise instrument because you see through it to the slides and pins doing the work.
UI: Black glass, one green, amber only at the limit. Corner brackets frame every readout cell; the mean flexion and effort sit top left and top right in 30pt Chakra Petch; the four fingers are ticked gauges bottom left with an effort trace bottom right. A radar rose with 72 ticks sits under the hologram. Navigation is a radial dial: three sectors on a 200 unit arc with a ticked outer scale and a needle from a hidden centre pointing at the sector you are in; tapping a sector swings the needle. Commands are bracketed cells that fill green when pressed. The joint matrix, the take list and the transport rise as dark panels with a hairline border over the lower half. Data lists every wire name over its reading and the four rates.
Different from every previous design because: 31 is enamel and keys, 32 paper and serif, 33 a blue sheet with tables; this is black with additive green light, a squared techno face, corner brackets instead of rules, and a dial instead of keys, rail or tabs. The twin is the only transparent one so far.
Why this is the best possible UI: an operator's display puts the numbers where the eyes already are, around the subject, and never over it. The hologram shows the interior of the mechanism, which no opaque material can. The dial is one gesture from anywhere.
What I would do better next time: The needle hides most of its length below the edge; a smaller radius would show more of it. The corner cells could carry sparklines. The Data panel hides the hologram almost completely.

---- DESIGN 35: Atelier ----
Thesis: A jewel on walnut: a polished gold-titanium hand in a pool of light, garamond, and one screen you swipe across and up, with no tabs and no keys.
Twin: Polished metal, which the exploration had never tried: members at metalness 0.95 and roughness 0.16 in a warm titanium gold, the palm a shade deeper, pins bright steel, encoder boards in black lacquer under a full clearcoat. A metal is only its reflections, so the environment is a built studio: a large warm softbox overhead, a tall cool strip behind right, a warm strip behind left, a dark floor and a walnut room, so every cylinder carries one warm highlight, one cool edge and dark turns between. ACES at 1.05. A low 24 degree lens at elevation 16, a slow wide swing (0.26 rad at 0.11 Hz), no ground: the hand hangs in a radial pool of light. It reads as a precise instrument because a mirror finish shows every facet of the machining and every slide as a distinct bright shape.
UI: One screen. The hand above, and beneath it three pages you swipe between (Live, Replay, Data) marked by three small diamonds; swiping up on a page opens its detail (the twelve joints as a hairline table), swiping down closes it. The welcome is a veil over the same screen: the name in 58pt Cormorant light and italic, a champagne medallion, "tap to begin". Cormorant Garamond throughout with its old-style figures; 64pt light for mean flexion, italics for every caption, tracked capitals at 10.5pt for labels. The four fingers are hairline rings carrying their initial. Controls are hairline medallions with drawn glyphs that fill champagne when pressed, and words with a gold dot beneath when chosen. The feed badge is a diamond and a word in the masthead.
Different from every previous design because: 31 to 34 all have a visible navigation structure (keys, rail, tabs, dial); this has none, only gestures and three diamonds. The twin is the first metal, the first with a built reflection studio, and the stage is the first warm one. The type is the first old-style serif with true italics at display size.
Why this is the best possible UI: the most expensive object in the app is the hand, and this treats it as one: nothing competes, every control is a hairline, and the navigation is what a hand does naturally on glass. The numbers are set like a menu at a good restaurant, which is exactly how much decoration a number needs.
What I would do better next time: The Data page is long inside a short pager and needs a taller open state. The rings could carry the flexion number on a long press. The pool of light should follow the hand's swing.

---- DESIGN 36: Clinic ----
Thesis: A medical device: porcelain on pale blue, vitals in tiles, an arc gauge with a normal band for every joint, and large sober tabs you cannot miss.
Twin: Glazed porcelain: white members at roughness 0.22 under a full clearcoat with the room environment mirrored in every curve, the palm one step greyer, navy encoder boards and steel pins for contrast. A high soft key with a 2048 shadow map casting one light shadow onto a pale blue ground, a cool fill from behind, a wide hemisphere so nothing is harsh. Neutral tone mapping. The stage is a rounded pale-blue panel with a hairline border, the way a monitor frames its waveform. It reads as a precise instrument because it looks sterilised: no colour on the object, all the colour in the boards and the pins.
UI: White ground, navy text, one calm blue, amber only for "outside the band" and green only for OK. A title bar with the screen name and two status chips (the feed, joints live). Vitals as monitor tiles: 30pt bold numbers with small caps labels; a tile tints amber when its value leaves the band. Fingers as bars with the top 18 percent drawn as an amber band; the twelve-joint detail is a grid of arc gauges (220 degrees, 7pt stroke, the band amber, a centre tick for the signed abduction). Replay is a list of session cards with a round blue play button, then a transport with a 60pt play button, a segmented speed control and a trend you drag. Data is a labelled form and two ruled tables with a status column. Tabs at the foot are 56pt with a drawn line icon and a word, the active one on a blue tint. Source Sans 3, 400/600/700.
Different from every previous design because: it is the first light UI with saturated colour, the first with a conventional tab bar (deliberately: a device UI must be learnable in seconds), and the first with tiles and gauges that carry a normal band. The twin is glazed and white where 31 was satin and 35 was metal.
Why this is the best possible UI: this is the design a clinician would sign off on. Every reading has a band, every state has a colour that means one thing, every target is a thumb wide, and nothing is decorative. Sobriety is a feature.
What I would do better next time: The porcelain reads slightly flat on pale blue; a darker rim or a cooler key would separate the fingers better. The mean-flexion tile could carry a sparkline. The Data table wraps in the Measures column.
