TAKTO ONE companion app: UI design exploration, designs 31 to 50
================================================================

This file is append-only. Once a design is committed its entry is frozen.
Mistakes are corrected in the next design, never covered up in a previous one.

LESSONS FROM 1-30
-----------------
What worked. One stage, one subject: the hand alone, nothing drawn on top of
it, and the detail hidden until asked for. The honest feed badge on every
screen. Light tabular numerals for the summary. The decision that the accent
is spent on state, not on decoration. The capture harness: a pinned clock and
a URL that pins the screen, so every still is reproducible.

What did not. The twin was flat: roughness 0.92, no clearcoat, no specular,
no contact shadow, and the ACES transform on top of a matte tone turned every
colour into the same dusty toy plastic. Thirty looks were thirty colour swaps
of that one recipe, and none of them changed the light, the lens or the
stage. The hand was also framed too small and too high, floating in a black
field with nothing to relate to. The chrome was weak: glass at 7 percent
white over black is a grey smudge, the round icon buttons could be from any
app, the picker chips were generic Feather icons in pills, the nav a three
icon capsule. Nothing said TAKTO. Nothing asked to be touched.

What the twin needs to read as a precise instrument on a phone. A lens, not
a game camera: 24 to 32 degrees, close, the hand filling at least half the
short axis. Real specular behaviour: a clearcoat or a metal, a roughness in
the 0.3 to 0.55 band, an environment to reflect, and a rim light separating
the silhouette from the stage. Material contrast INSIDE the object: the
finger members in one material, the encoder boards and pins in a second, the
palm block in a third, so the mechanism reads as assembled parts rather than
as a single cast blob. A ground relationship: a contact shadow, a reflection,
a grid, a horizon, or a paper edge, so the hand has somewhere to be. Motion
that means something: the twin moves because the joints moved.

What I will do differently. Every design starts from the twin: material,
light, camera and stage are decided and looked at before any chrome is
drawn. Every design changes stage, type, colour, layout AND navigation, not
one of them. Controls are drawn as objects a hand recognises (a key, a
dial, a toggle, a rail, a tab of paper, a bezel) with real contrast and a
real pressed state, never a translucent pill with an icon in it. Fonts are
chosen per design and Inter is not used again. Every still is read at full
size before it is committed.

---- DESIGN 31: Rams panel ----
Thesis: A machine hand is an instrument, so show it the way Braun showed a radio: in a window cut into warm grey enamel, with keys you could find with your eyes closed.
Twin: Satin white members (roughness 0.34-0.40, clearcoat 0.5) with dark steel pins and graphite encoder boards, so the mechanism reads as assembled parts. A softbox environment high left, one hard warm key with a 2048 shadow map, a cool strip rim behind right, a floor bounce. The hand stands on a shadow-catching ground in a recessed window the colour of the panel, one step darker, with an inset shade along its top edge. A 27 degree lens at 1.9 units, elevation 30, and the new fitAspect rule so a short window moves the camera in rather than losing the hand. It reads as an instrument because it is photographed like a product: one key, one rim, one shadow, and its own contrasting parts.
UI: A panel below the window. A status strip with an amber LED and the words SYNTHETIC FEED on every screen. Keys with a face, a 2px side and a soft fall, that push down when pressed; the power key is charcoal with a green LED; three wide keys at the foot are the navigation. Mean flexion is a red needle on a printed scale beside a 34pt number; the four fingers and effort are segmented level meters with a red band at the top; the JOINTS key flips the panel into a 4x3 matrix of arc dials with needles that have mass (spring). Replay is a tape transport: a printed effort trace with a red playhead you drag, PAUSE as the dark key, EJECT, a ridged rotary with three detents for speed. Data prints the wire names on value tiles and the four rates as a table. Archivo throughout, small, tracked, upper case for silkscreen, tabular numerals.
Different from every previous design because: closest to the baseline in subject only. The stage is a light enamel panel, not black; nothing is translucent; the controls are physical keys, meters, needles and a rotary rather than glass pills; navigation is a key row; the type is Archivo not Inter; the twin is a white product shot with a real shadow, not a matte tone floating on black.
Why this is the best possible UI: every reading is an instrument you already know how to read (scale, meter, dial, tape), every control is a thing you already know how to press, and the hand is shown as what it is: a white precision product on a table. It ages well; nothing here depends on a trend.
What I would do better next time: The welcome leaves a band of empty panel under the keys. The dials could carry printed limits at the red band. Skip glyph keys need drawn triangles, not text characters.
